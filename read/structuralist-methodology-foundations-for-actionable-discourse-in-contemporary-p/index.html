Abstract

Academic institutions can recognize and reproduce philosophical work independently of its capacity to alter, predict, or intervene in the systems it describes. The prevalence of non-actionable discourse is a question for empirical audit. This report introduces an output-based operational classification of philosophical inquiry, establishes the distinction between institutional recognition and actionable capability, and evaluates theoretical frameworks by what their investigations enable rather than how their traditions classify themselves.

This report defines an Execution Vector (EV) as the actionable result of academic discourse. Discourse with no such result is classified as non-actionable regardless of its terminology or lineage. Under the principles of scoped functional equivalence established in Phi Labs’ Structuralist Methodology (SM-050), distinct intellectual traditions that produce equivalently null execution vectors are functionally equivalent with respect to actionability (A ≅_EV B). This paper demonstrates why Structuralist Methodology does not supersede post-postmodernism or metamodernism by historical succession; rather, it performs a fundamentally different investigative operation, resolving systems into constituent entities, relationships, interfaces, constraints, and authority configurations to derive verifiable interventions.
