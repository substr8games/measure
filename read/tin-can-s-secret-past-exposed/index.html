TIN CAN'S SECRET PAST EXPOSED

Unlabeled Witness Refuses to Deny Allegations of Being Full of Beans

BY Phi Labs Investigates   /   2026-11-23

OPEN INTERACTIVE EDITION ↗

THE MEASURE | SPECIAL INVESTIGATION | 3:00 AM

An unidentified tin can was forcibly removed from a courtroom yesterday following a contentious hearing that has raised serious questions about the witness's credibility, personal history, and possible involvement with several other canned goods.

The witness, whose contents remain unknown, was reportedly subjected to intense questioning before being found in contempt of court and removed through a rear exit by a milk carton serving as bailiff.

"It doesn't matter that the bailiff is a milk carton, but it totally does."

The Measure has obtained photographs of the incident showing the witness being dragged from the courtroom by one arm, apparently unconscious. Court officials have declined to explain the circumstances surrounding the removal, although several observers described the proceedings as "heated."

The courtroom was equipped with multiple heat lamps.

It is not clear whether the lamps played any role in the incident.

A Troubling History of Non-Disclosure

Sources familiar with the pantry describe the witness as "metallic," "difficult to open," and "possibly expired."

The can has no visible label, no publicly available ingredients list, and no documented history of refrigeration. Investigators have also been unable to establish its place of manufacture, original purchase price, or whether its contents are suitable for human consumption.

Perhaps most troubling, the witness has produced no documentation concerning its childhood.

The Measure contacted several individuals familiar with the can's early life. None could confirm where it was born, how it was raised, or whether it had ever been anything other than a can.

These revelations raise serious questions about the witness's suitability to testify under oath.

Questions Surrounding the Bailiff

The involvement of a milk carton has attracted particular scrutiny.

While court officials maintain that the bailiff was acting within its authority, photographs appear to show the carton administering an electrical shock to the witness shortly before its removal.

The milk carton has not been charged with misconduct.

The Measure has independently confirmed that milk cartons are generally manufactured from coated paperboard, a fact which court officials have neither acknowledged nor disputed.

Whether the bailiff's packaging influenced its conduct remains unknown.

Associations Raise Further Concerns

An investigation into the witness's personal relationships has uncovered a pattern of potentially troubling associations.

The can was reportedly seen in the company of several other shelf-stable products, some of which have since disappeared from the pantry.

One individual familiar with the arrangement described the group as "mostly vegetables."

The Measure has been unable to determine whether these relationships were personal, commercial, or part of a broader storage arrangement.

More concerning still, an anonymous source claims the witness was seen near a can opener as recently as Tuesday.

When asked whether the encounter was consensual, the source declined to speculate.

No evidence of wrongdoing has been established, although the absence of evidence has done little to resolve the questions surrounding the witness.

A Pattern of Evasion

Throughout yesterday's proceedings, the witness repeatedly failed to provide satisfactory answers regarding its contents.

At one point, counsel reportedly demanded to know whether the can contained beans.

The witness refused to answer.

It is worth emphasizing that the witness has never publicly denied containing beans.

The Measure has consulted several individuals with experience opening canned goods. All agreed that the contents of an unopened can are difficult to establish with certainty.

One expert, speaking on condition of anonymity, suggested that the matter could be resolved by opening the can.

Court officials have not indicated whether such a procedure is under consideration.

The Public Deserves Answers

As questions continue to mount, the witness's refusal to disclose basic facts about its identity has become increasingly difficult to reconcile with its insistence on being treated as a credible participant in judicial proceedings.

The Measure remains committed to investigating this matter until the public has been provided with a satisfactory accounting of the witness's contents, associations, and activities.

We have not independently verified any allegations concerning the can.

Nevertheless, the sheer number of unanswered questions suggests that further investigation may be warranted.

The Measure has requested all available court records, pantry inventories, purchase receipts, and expiration documentation. We have also sought comment from the milk carton, several nearby condiments, and a box of crackers that may have witnessed the incident.

None have responded.

The box of crackers was reportedly empty.

The tin can has declined to comment to The Measure but maintains its innocence.
