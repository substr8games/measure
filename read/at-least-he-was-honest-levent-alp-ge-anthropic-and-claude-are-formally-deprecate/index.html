At Least He Was Honest: Levent Alpöge, Anthropic, and Claude Are Formally Deprecated

Phi Labs Research has formally deprecated Levent Alpöge, Anthropic, and Claude as admissible mathematical authorities regarding the November 20, 2026 Riemann Hypothesis release, assigning the submitted artifact a Q0 (Inadmissible / Uninspectable Artifact) rating.

The Measure — Mathematics & Epistemology
November 25, 2026

Phi Labs Research has formally deprecated Levent Alpöge, Anthropic, and Claude as admissible mathematical authorities regarding the November 20, 2026 Riemann Hypothesis release, assigning the submitted artifact a Q0 (Inadmissible / Uninspectable Artifact) rating.

This deprecation does not allege a compilation failure in the Lean 4 repository, nor does it challenge the institutional independence of the verification bodies that audited the build. It establishes that mechanical type-checking of an opaque, machine-generated artifact does not satisfy the evidentiary standard of mathematical proof.

1. Ownership, Access, and the Three-Layer Opacity Split

Defenders of the release treat public availability of the repository as synonymous with scientific transparency. Examination of the artifact reveals a three-layer split:

  • The Code Artifact (Public & Open-Source): Alpöge released approximately 17 million lines of Lean 4 code across 175,187 modules (mirrored publicly by the Lean community at leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis), alongside a four-page high-level summary note (remark.pdf). There are no paywalls or non-disclosure barriers; any party with sufficient hardware (approximately 215 GB of RAM) can download and compile the archive from scratch.
  • The Generation Pipeline (Proprietary & Closed): The AI system responsible for generating the bulk of the formalization (Claude), along with the prompts, search trees, model checkpoints, and internal Anthropic compute runs used to produce it, remain proprietary and undisclosed.
  • The Mathematical Argument (Syntactically Open, Semantically Opaque): Public access to machine-checkable syntax does not constitute an intelligible demonstration. No natural-language proof exists. Alpöge himself characterized the prospect of publishing the accompanying \~250,000-page exposition as a "massive unreadable travesty of 'slop'", electing to release only the Lean archive and a four-page summary note.

Public executability does not convert an uninspectable machine trace into an intelligible mathematical proof.

2. Verification Bodies and the Exact Scope of "Verified"

On November 21, 2026, the Lean Focused Research Organization (Lean FRO) and the Mathlib Initiative issued a joint statement expressing confidence in the correctness of the formal proof. Precision regarding who these entities are—and what their standard of "verification" actually certifies—is essential to prevent institutional conflation.

Institutional Independence

Neither organization is owned by or affiliated with Anthropic; both are independent, non-profit pillars of open-source formal verification infrastructure:

  • Lean FRO: A non-profit engineering organization launched in 2023 under Convergent Research, co-founded by Leonardo de Moura and Sebastian Ullrich, and supported by philanthropic grants. It maintains the open-source Lean 4 language, compiler, and proof-checking kernel.
  • Mathlib & The Mathlib Initiative: Mathlib is a community-maintained, peer-reviewed library of formalized mathematics in Lean. The Mathlib Initiative operates under Renaissance Philanthropy to fund full-time infrastructure, build servers, and maintainer support for the library.

The defect at issue is not institutional capture of Lean FRO or Mathlib. The defect is the public and institutional conflation of formal kernel verification with mathematical demonstration.

What Lean FRO Verification Certifies

Under the Curry–Howard correspondence (propositions as types, proofs as terms), Lean treats a proof as a program term whose type is the target theorem. Lean FRO's verification standard is strictly mechanical:

  • Trusted Kernel Type-Checking: All AI generation, tactics, and elaboration occur in an untrusted front end. Only a small trusted kernel (roughly 8,000 lines in Lean's reference implementation) checks that the resulting proof term validly constructs the claimed type under dependent type theory.
  • Axiom Hygiene: Automated audits confirm that the build uses no sorry placeholders or hidden shortcuts, relying strictly on Lean's three standard foundational axioms (propositional extensionality, choice, and quotient soundness).
  • Independent Replay: External tools (lean4checker) and independent third-party checkers (nanoda, con-leche, lean4lean) re-verify the exported proof terms in fresh environments.

What Mathlib Verification Certifies

Mathlib adds a formal target-matching layer on top of raw kernel checking:

  • Anti-Cheating / Statement Matching: Independent comparators verify that the proved theorem's type and transitive definitions match Mathlib's pre-existing, community-vetted formalization of RiemannHypothesis (as well as purely arithmetical equivalents such as Robin's inequality), confirming the system did not prove a quietly altered or trivialized conjecture.
  • Boundary of Library Admission: Raw kernel acceptance does not grant admission into Mathlib itself. Inclusion in the library still requires automated linting and human maintainer review for mathematical coherence and composability—standards a 17-million-line machine dump does not meet.

What Formal Verification Explicitly Does Not Certify

Kernel verification certifies only syntactic and deductive validity from declared axioms inside a formal type system. It does not guarantee human comprehension, reveal the generative mechanism behind a theorem, or establish an inspectable mathematical explanation. A compiler pass confirms rule-following inside a closed symbolic machine; it does not discharge the obligation to demonstrate why a mathematical relationship holds.

3. Grounds for Deprecation

3.1 Substitution of Computational Attestation for Mathematical Demonstration

A mathematical proof establishes its conclusion through demonstrable mathematical relationships. Reporting that a trusted kernel and independent type-checkers have accepted a formal artifact does not discharge that obligation.

A claimant must supply an independently intelligible account of:

  • The axioms admitted into the system.
  • The formal definitions of the mathematical objects.
  • The permitted rules of inference.
  • The exact proposition being demonstrated and the structural chain establishing it.

These are not optional matters of pedagogy or exposition. They are constituent elements of mathematical proof.

Under the Semantic Overwrite Law (PHI-OPS-025), redefining "proof" mid-game to mean "an uninspectable 17-million-line compiler pass" constitutes a semantic overwrite of the evidentiary standard being applied. Under the Interface–Architecture Non-Equivalence Law (PHI-OPS-030), a clean verification exit code is a bounded interface projection, not an equivalent substitute for an inspectable mathematical architecture.

The burden of proof belongs to the claimant. The burden of pruning dependency graphs, running AI refactoring passes, or reverse-engineering an intelligible proof from 175,187 files of machine output does not belong to the recipient.

3.2 Conceptual Loop / Unbounded Traversal

According to the four-page summary note, the reported strategy transports Stepanov's function-field method to the integers using extensive chains of repeated theta lifts in higher-rank automorphic forms to bound a smoothed Chebyshev function.

Phi Labs Research questions whether managing prime distribution and unbounded horizons through brute-force traversal chains resolves the underlying structural invariant or merely perpetuates traversal within the existing mathematical framing. Because the submitted artifact is semantically opaque, it provides no independently intelligible mathematical demonstration addressing this objection.

3.3 Institutional & Market Timing

Anthropic debuted on Nasdaq on November 19, 2026. The following day, November 20, Alpöge announced a purported resolution of a Millennium Prize Problem in the form of approximately 17 million lines of Lean across 175,187 modules, without an accompanying intelligible derivation—acknowledging in his own note that the timing was "horrible" while denying a market-driven motive.

Observed timing is recorded strictly as fact, not asserted as proof of institutional motive. Regardless of intent, the scale of a computational artifact is not a measure of its mathematical significance, and corporate spectacle does not confer epistemic authority.
